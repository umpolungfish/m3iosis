The compiler scores gates up to phase, and the recursion contracts.

The braid representation is projective, so scoring against the raw Frobenius norm charged for an unobservable global phase and reported a wrong gate as badly wrong; quotienting the phase alone took the two-gate circuit from over half the maximum possible error down to a tenth of it. The dictionary enumerated words rather than distinct gates, so its covering radius stalled regardless of depth, and a breadth-first net keyed on the projective value reaches a radius small enough for the group-commutator recursion to start from. That recursion then amplified rather than contracted because the lift to the special unitary group was not sign-fixed, reading a rotation by a twentieth of a radian as one by nearly a full turn.
